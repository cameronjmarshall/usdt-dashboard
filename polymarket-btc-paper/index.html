How this experiment works

The assumptions behind every number.

PAPER ONLY

Entries, exits & expiry

Bots 1 and 2 buy at 35¢ or less before 2:30, then only sell. Bot 1 targets 70¢; Bot 2 targets 85¢. Bot 3 buys at 40¢ or less before 2:00, then at 45¢ or less from 2:00 to 3:00, targeting 60¢ throughout. No target exits at or after 5:00. Bot 4 makes one $10 entry attempt at the first fresh snapshot crossing 2:30 if exactly one side has an ask strictly above 80¢, then holds to settlement. Bot 5 uses the same timing and $10 fee-inclusive budget, but requires 80¢ < ask ≤ 90¢ and caps every fill level at 90¢ before fees. Bot 6 uses the same rules with an 85¢ cap on every fill. Bots 4–6 hold until official settlement and skip the round if the checkpoint is missed, paused, unfunded, or has insufficient depth; none retries later. Bots 5 and 6 are forward trials of their price caps, with separate paper balances and no historical backfill.

Unsold shares wait for an official outcome and settle at $1 or $0. No forced sale at the midpoint or expiry, and no stop loss. Each bot can trade a new market while earlier positions await settlement. Their stakes remain reserved, and new entries require enough available cash.

Fills, liquidity & costs

Buys use available asks at or below the entry limit; sells use bids at or above the target. The entire order must fit in displayed depth. For Bots 1–3, two qualifying observations at least one second apart are required, with at most a five-second gap. Stale and crossed books cannot fill. Bots 4–6 use one checkpoint observation, with a fresh preceding observation within five seconds and execution less than five seconds after 2:30.

Each bot independently tests the same liquidity. This models marketable taker orders, not resting maker orders or a shared portfolio. REST polling can miss brief moves. Actual fills, queue competition and partial executions can differ.

Fee metadata is loaded with each market.

Results & persistence

Closed P&L and win rate include both target sales and settled winners/losers. Open equity uses the latest observed bids, after estimated exit fees; insufficient depth is valued at zero for the unfilled remainder. Stale estimates are labelled. Drawdown uses closed P&L only.

The collector continues while this Python process runs, even if the browser closes. SQLite saves observations and trades. Stopping the process or putting the computer to sleep creates a data gap; missed trades are not reconstructed.
